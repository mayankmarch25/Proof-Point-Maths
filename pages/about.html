  • MSc Applied Mathematics, in progress

  • UGC-NET qualified, 97.7 percentile
  • PMP and GARP SCR certified
  • Practice Leader, HFS Research

I did not leave a job to start a tuition centre. I have a demanding one, and I intend to keep it. That is exactly why this place is built the way it is.

On weekdays I lead healthcare and life sciences research at HFS Research, a global analyst firm. The work is quantitative: survey data, market models, scoring frameworks, and advising senior executives at US and Indian healthcare organisations on decisions worth a great deal of money. Before that I built software for medical devices at HCL and advised CXOs at Altimetrik.

Alongside that I am completing an MSc in Applied Mathematics, and I hold UGC-NET qualification, which makes me eligible to teach at university level. I also write a weekly newsletter read by healthcare operators and investors, built almost entirely on public data that most people never open.

None of that would matter to your child, except for one thing. Two decades of using mathematics to make real decisions changes how you teach it. I have never once needed to recall a formula I did not understand. I have very often needed to know why a method exists, where it breaks, and whether the number in front of me can be trusted. That is the maths I teach.

Why weekends only, stated plainly

Because I teach every batch myself. Many centres sell you the founder and hand your child to a rotating junior tutor by the third week. I cannot do that, because there is nobody else here. The cost of that honesty is that classes run only on Saturday and Sunday. The benefit is that the person who assessed your child in the diagnostic is the same person standing in front of them in month six.

Why I started it

I kept meeting bright students who had decided they were bad at maths. Almost none of them were. They had one unclosed gap, usually two or three years old, and every chapter after it was built on top of that gap. Nobody had gone back to look. That is a solvable problem, and it does not need a bigger institute. It needs someone willing to sit with twelve students and check.

What I will not do

No batch larger than twelve. No teaching to the answer key. No telling a parent their child is improving without a test score to show for it. No taking a student whose gap is better closed elsewhere, in which case I will say so on the enquiry call and not charge you for finding out.
